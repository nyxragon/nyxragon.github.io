Our whole lives live on our phones now. Every photo, every private moment, every version of us that was only ever meant for our own eyes, or for someone we trusted. The more of our lives move online, the more there is to leak, steal, or fake. Every year it gets worse, not better.

Maybe you've already heard of it happening to someone. A video that was never meant to leave two phones, suddenly on a page full of strangers. Private photos, leaked on purpose. And more and more, it isn't even real to begin with. Someone's face stitched onto another body, a video of a moment that never happened. Fake, but it still lands on a real person.

The mainstream platforms have real rules against this now, and they'll take it down when it's reported. But the danger is everywhere else: the shady corners of the internet, the pages that don't care and solely exist to dump people's private lives for clicks. It can happen to someone you know, or, god forbid, even you, from a toxic ex looking for revenge, someone you trusted, or just a random person who gets something out of making another human being miserable.

And what it does to people is brutal: the shame, the embarrassment, the depression. In the worst cases it has cost people their lives. The cruelest part is that most won't ask for help, drowning in shame, quietly assuming that going to the police will only make it slower, messier, more humiliating than it already is. But whoever did this wanted you to feel small and ashamed. That was the whole point of it. Don't hand it to them. You are bigger than the worst thing someone tried to do to you, and standing tall through this is the one thing they never see coming.

---

Before any of the options below, two things are worth knowing, and both are easy to miss.

> **The law is already on your side.** In India, once you (or someone on your behalf) file a complaint with the platform, any platform operating in India is required to remove or block non-consensual intimate content, and fast: **within 2 hours. No court order, no lawyer, no money.** ([IT Rules, Rule 3(2)(b), amended Feb 2026, G.S.R. 120(E)](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/269993.pdf)) It's a right, not a favor.

The other is to **save the proof first.** Before reporting anything, capture it: screenshot the posts and messages, and save the web addresses and the dates. It can feel wrong to hold on to something you want gone, but that record is what every step below runs on.

Everything else (where to report, how each option works, who can help) is laid out below. The legal steps here are India's, but the tools to report and remove it work anywhere.

## 1. Stop it spreading: StopNCII

**[StopNCII](https://stopncii.org) helps get your image or video taken down across partner platforms, and the file never leaves your device to do it.** That's the part that trips people up, so here's how it works: it turns that file into a **hash**, a unique fingerprint of that exact image or video, a long string of numbers. Only that fingerprint is sent, never the file itself.

That fingerprint goes to every platform partnered with StopNCII, including:

```chips
Pornhub, OnlyFans, Facebook, Instagram, Threads, TikTok, Snapchat, Reddit, X, and more
```

When someone uploads an image or video that matches, the platform can flag it, review it and take it down. That works whether the image is already out there or hasn't been posted yet: if someone is only threatening to leak it, you can fingerprint it now so partner platforms can catch it if it's ever uploaded.

**It covers fakes too: you just get the file a different way.** If it's a real photo or video, you probably already have the original, so use that. If it's a deepfake, there's no original, but StopNCII only needs a copy to fingerprint, so save the fake from wherever it's posted. And if you can't get a copy at all, because it's only being threatened or somewhere you can't reach, there's nothing to hash yet, so lean on [platform reports](#4-get-it-removed-platforms-adult-sites-messaging-apps) and [the law](#3-the-2-hour-law-and-how-to-report-it-officially-india) instead.

> As of 2026, StopNCII has been widening its reach across the services people actually use. Microsoft now [matches StopNCII's fingerprints](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2026/05/27/strengthening-our-approach-to-tackling-non-consensual-intimate-imagery/) across **Teams Free, OneDrive and Xbox**, and **Google** has [announced it will use them](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/stopncii-program-partnership/) to keep matching images out of **Search** results, on top of the mainstream apps and major adult sites it already covers.
>
> → **Start a case at [stopncii.org](https://stopncii.org)**

**The honest part:** StopNCII only reaches platforms that have partnered with it, not the countless no-name sites. It matches the same image: if someone crops, filters or re-edits the file, that altered version may need a new fingerprint. This bites hardest with video, which StopNCII matches exactly, so a clipped video can slip through; its own fix is to also hash a few screenshots of the video. So it's a strong first layer, not the whole wall. A few other options work alongside it to widen that reach further: [the law](#3-the-2-hour-law-and-how-to-report-it-officially-india), [search removal](#5-remove-from-search-engines), and [the people who help](#6-people-who-can-help). StopNCII is for adults 18 and over; if the case involves someone under 18, see the segment below.

> ### If you are or were under 18 in the image, use Take It Down instead
>
> **Take It Down is built for this.** Same idea as StopNCII: the image or video never leaves your device, only a fingerprint does. It's run by [NCMEC](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org) and covers anyone who is under 18, or was when the image or video was taken.
>
> → **Start at [takeitdown.ncmec.org](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org)** · free, anonymous

## 2. If you're being threatened, before anything's posted

Sometimes nothing has been posted yet. Someone is **threatening to leak** an image or video, or demanding money or more photos to stay quiet. That's sextortion, and it's one of the most frightening situations there is, because the whole thing runs on your panic.

Here's what to do while you're in it, drawn from the [FBI, NCMEC](https://www.justice.gov/usao-or/pr/fbi-and-partners-issue-national-public-safety-alert-financial-sextortion-schemes) and survivor helplines:

```steps
Don't pay, and don't send more | Paying marks you as someone who is willing to pay. It rarely stops the blackmail, and it often brings more demands.
Stop replying, but keep everything | Block them, but don't delete the chat or their profile. That's your evidence.
Save the proof first | Screenshots of the threats, their username and profile link, any UPI/crypto demand, and the dates.
Fingerprint the image or video now, before it's posted | StopNCII (18+) or Take It Down (under 18) let partner platforms catch it in advance ([section 1](#1-stop-it-spreading-stopncii)). The single best pre-emptive move.
Report it | You're reporting a crime, not confessing to one. File the threat at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in), and if it's ongoing, loop in your local police. If the person being threatened is under 18, also call **Childline 1098** (or **112** in an emergency).
!If you've already paid, act fast | Call **1930** right away. It can help freeze a bank or UPI transfer before it's gone.
Tell one person you trust | You don't have to carry the fear alone, and it stops you acting out of panic.
```

**The honest part:** these threats run on scripts: "pay now or I send it to everyone in your contacts." The same copy-paste lines get used on victim after victim, and paying doesn't buy safety: the FBI warns that in many cases they release the images even after being paid. Whoever it is, the moves above still hold. Not paying and reporting is the strongest thing you can do. And if someone does post, you're still far from powerless: the fingerprint already helps partner platforms catch matching copies, the law is on your side, and [the people who help for a living](#6-people-who-can-help) handle exactly this, every day.

> **When a teenager is the one being targeted:** it's a crime committed against them, never something they did, and they're **not in trouble**. It's far more common than most people realize: in 2025, [NCMEC logged more than 50,000 reports](https://us.missingkids.org/blog/2026/ncmec-releases-new-sextortion-data-2025) of financial sextortion, about 137 a day. Shame and secrecy are the blackmailer's biggest weapons. The single most protective thing anyone can do is **tell a trusted adult fast**.

## 3. The 2-hour law, and how to report it officially (India)

**You have a legal right to have this removed fast, and you don't need a court, a lawyer, or even the police to use it.**

```facts
2 hours | the takedown deadline
No court | no judge or order
No lawyer | file it yourself
Free | no cost at all
```

Under India's IT Rules, once the affected person (or someone acting for them) complains, the platform must take down intimate or impersonated/morphed content, including videos, **within 2 hours**, a window cut from 24 hours by the [IT Rules Amendment 2026, G.S.R. 120(E)](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/269993.pdf), in force since 20 February 2026. (MeitY's [NCII takedown SOP](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/a2c9500ef5f8b62a43bfc68747de592d.pdf) came out before that change and still says 24 hours; the 2-hour rule is what applies now.) If they don't, they can lose their legal protection.

*If it's AI-made or morphed:* it's still covered here. The 2-hour rule already covers "artificially morphed images" of you, and the 2026 amendment separately names non-consensual intimate imagery as something AI tools must block. India has no separate "deepfake law" yet, and that's not a gap. It just means a fake is handled with the *same* tools as a real image or video.

The official place to report is the **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal** ([cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in)), open to everyone (there's also a [direct social-media report form](https://cybercrime.gov.in/Webform/report_abuse_social_media.aspx)).

If you complained to the platform's grievance officer and it ignored you or said no, you're not stuck. You can appeal to the government's [Grievance Appellate Committee](https://gac.gov.in).

**The law here protects everyone, regardless of gender.** The 2-hour takedown right and the IT Act's core offences are written for "an individual" and "any person," so they're yours to use whoever you are (a few other criminal sections, like voyeurism, cover only women). And for anyone LGBTQ+, your sexuality is not a crime: consensual same-sex relationships have been legal in India since the Supreme Court's [2018 Navtej Johar ruling](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/168671544/), so reporting doesn't expose you for it.

> In 2025, a [Madras High Court order](https://images.assettype.com/barandbench/2025-07-09/57slnwqt/X_v_Union_of_India___Ors.pdf) directed the government to find, remove and block a woman's leaked videos across every platform, and kept the case open until the government produced a formal procedure. That procedure is MeitY's NCII takedown SOP.
>
> → **File at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in)**

**The honest part:** a complaint here is usually filed under your name, not anonymously. The **1930** helpline, meanwhile, is primarily the *financial-fraud* line. The government's NCII SOP now also lists it as a route for these cases, but for an actual takedown, the portal is the more reliable door.

## 5. Remove from search engines

**Even if a copy still exists somewhere, you can stop people from *finding* it by removing it from search results.** This cuts off the main way most people would ever stumble onto it.

- **Google.** Remove intimate images or videos of you, real *or* AI/deepfake, through Google's dedicated process: start on its [explicit-imagery removal help page](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16305143), then choose "Start removal request" to reach [the form](https://support.google.com/legal/contact/lr_idmec). You give the links and screenshots; once the request is granted, Google also tries to catch and remove duplicates. Google can also **demote** sites that get many removal requests, including sites with ["exploitative removal practices"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) (sites that charge people to remove content), pushing the rest of their content down in results.
- **Bing / Microsoft.** Report non-consensual intimate imagery at [microsoft.com/digitalsafety/report-a-concern](https://www.microsoft.com/digitalsafety/report-a-concern). Microsoft also uses [StopNCII](https://stopncii.org) fingerprints to detect it and remove it from Bing image results.

**The honest part:** search removal works best paired with the takedown steps above. Together, the takedowns get it *down*, while search removal stops people from *finding* it. On its own, hiding it from search doesn't remove it from the site that still hosts it, but when a site won't budge, making it hard to find is often the biggest win there is. (Once Google removes something, it also tries to catch duplicates; [more on watching for re-uploads](#7-protect-yourself-and-watch-for-re-uploads).)

## 7. Protect yourself, and watch for re-uploads

Whether this has already happened or you just want to be ready, a few things genuinely lower the risk and help you stay ahead.

- **Fingerprint pre-emptively.** If you fear a specific image or video could leak, register it with [StopNCII or Take It Down](#1-stop-it-spreading-stopncii) *now*, so partner platforms can detect and remove it if anyone tries to share it.
- **Watch for re-uploads.** Set a [Google Alert](https://www.google.com/alerts) on your name as an early tripwire: it emails you when a new web page or news story mentions your name. It works on words like your name, not on images, so it won't spot your photo itself, closed groups, or paywalled posts, but it's an easy way to hear about a new copy early. And once you've [removed something through Google](#5-remove-from-search-engines), it also tries to catch duplicates.
- **If a hacked account or device was the way in, close that door.** A compromised email, cloud backup, or social account can be how it gets out, so change your passwords, turn on two-factor, sign out any sessions you don't recognize, and lock down your backups. Hiding your friends and followers list also removes a blackmailer's favorite lever: "I'll send it to everyone you know." *(Most leaks aren't a hack, though. They're usually someone the person trusted, a stranger pretending to be someone else, or a fake made from public photos, so this is one piece, not a shield.)*

## 8. How to help someone going through this

**If you're reading this for someone else, you might be the most important person in this whole story.** You just need to be a pillar of support. Here's what actually helps.

- **Start with what you say.** The first words matter more than any link: *"I believe you. It's not your fault. I'm here."* Never ask *"why did you send it / take it?"* There is no version of this where that helps.
- **Don't take over. Stand beside.** Ask before doing things for them. Offer to sit with them while they file a report or start a StopNCII case. Help secure the evidence. Let them take control of their own situation.
- **If it's your kid:** lead with *"you're not in trouble, and I'm glad you told me"*. Never threaten to take their phone or punish them. Shame, and the fear of getting in trouble or losing their phone, are what keep many young people silent while a blackmailer escalates. Say it to them *long before* it could ever happen.
- **Point them to the right door.** You don't have to know the details. You just have to point them in the right direction, toward the [people and services above](#6-people-who-can-help) who handle this every day. Coming from *you*, someone they trust, is often what makes them willing to reach out at all.

The point isn't that someone finds help alone in the middle of a crisis. It's that the people around them already know what to do before it ever happens. If you've read this calmly today, you've done something real.

> Whether you're here for yourself or for someone you love, hold on to this: the tools are real, the help is real, and no one has to face any of this by themselves. What was done was meant to make someone feel small, ashamed, and alone, **but it doesn't get the last word.**
