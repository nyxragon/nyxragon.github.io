A **T-shaped engineer** is someone with deep expertise in one or two areas (the vertical bar of the T) and broad knowledge across many other disciplines (the horizontal bar). The concept has become a popular way to think about career growth, especially in tech and security.

## The Vertical Bar: Depth

The vertical bar represents your core specialization—the area where you can go deep. In cybersecurity, that might be:

- Web application security
- Cloud security
- Red team operations
- Secure development (DevSecOps)
- Threat intelligence

This is where you build credibility, solve hard problems, and become the person others turn to for guidance.

## The Horizontal Bar: Breadth

The horizontal bar is your ability to collaborate across domains. You don't need to be an expert everywhere, but you need enough context to:

- Understand how systems fit together
- Communicate with developers, ops, and business teams
- Spot risks at the boundaries between systems
- Adapt when the landscape changes

## Why It Matters in Security

Security work rarely happens in isolation. You're constantly bridging:

- **Engineering** – understanding how code and infrastructure are built
- **Operations** – how things run in production
- **Business** – what actually matters to the organization
- **Compliance & risk** – how security fits into broader goals

A T-shaped mindset helps you stay effective as tools, threats, and roles evolve.

## Building Your T

- **Pick a vertical** – Double down on one area first. Get really good at it.
- **Learn adjacent skills** – Scripting, networking, cloud basics, or app development.
- **Say yes to cross-team work** – Projects that span teams stretch your horizontal bar.
- **Teach and document** – Explaining what you know forces clarity and exposes gaps.

The goal isn't to know everything. It's to be deep where it counts and broad enough to connect the dots.
